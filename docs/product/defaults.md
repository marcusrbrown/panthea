Status: Delegated product defaults under D23.
Rows marked Accepted summarize the explicit round-nine choices.
Rows marked Default make remaining details concrete for implementation and can be tuned with recorded evidence.

| Memory | Each actor keeps at most 24 memories (`memoryBalance.capacity`); a full memory forgets the least salient entry first and the oldest among equals, so live play and a rebuild from the log forget the same things. Witnessing is judged by the perception rule, at the moment of the event. Salience per event kind (`salience_<kind>`): building damaged 5, ignited 8, destroyed 9, repaired 4, worship 4, a belief formed from a report or a legend 4, a sign (a god's answer or silence) 6, a theft someone was there to see 6, and a loss a mortal noticed 5 (a legend is remembered by its hearers as told, never as witnessed, so it has no witnessed salience); every other kind is not remembered (routine gathering, trade, income, and movement stay out so the log and each memory hold what mattered). Set from the M2 unattended run | Default |
| Relationships | Harm one remembers lowers affinity toward whoever did it by 2 and adds a grudge when the rememberer was the one wronged; a kindness raises the served party's affinity by 1; a belief moves affinity by half as much as witnessing (`toldShare`, at least 1). Affinity stays within plus or minus 10 (`affinityLimit`) and a grudge within 10 (`grudgeLimit`). Affinity, however high, makes no alliance: only a sealed settlement does, by an accepted alliance term, in both directions (`allianceAffinity` is still an accepted key and does nothing). All in `memoryBalance`, checked key by key at content parse and again when a stored world decodes: counts and saliences are whole numbers of at least 0, `toldShare` is the one fraction, an unknown key is refused (a typo would silently leave the default), and a stored world may not hold more memories than its capacity or a relationship beyond its limits | Default |
| Rumors | A report carries the teller's words (at most 280 characters, `MAX_REPORT_LENGTH`) and an optional structured claim: who did what to whom, which the teller asserts and may be false. The rules check the claim's shape and that the ids exist, never its truth. A listener's belief takes its subjects and consequence only from the claim, attributed to the teller; the event a report cites is provenance only, and only an actor that witnessed that event may cite it (one hop: someone who was only told may retell the story, uncited, as their own account). No claim means a story and no relationship change. A listener takes a given account from a given teller once: if it already holds a told memory from that teller with the same claim (or, for a story with no claim, the same words), the repeat forms no memory and no feeling, so repeating a report cannot farm a relationship. A belief the listener has forgotten can be told again. The world never rewrites the teller's words or resolves a belief to the truth | Default |
| Legends | A legend is told aloud to everyone present at the narrator's place when it commits: the living actors there, minus the narrator, fixed by the rules at execution and recorded on the event, so someone who arrives later never hears it, and one told to an empty place is still a legend. Each hearer holds it as a belief attributed to the narrator, through the same path as a report, with the same claim dedup and feelings. A legend's text is bounded like a report's (280 characters), and its optional claim is checked for shape and existing ids like a report's. It does not spread by itself: a hearer passes it on only by a report. It changes no divinity, favor, or worship | Default |
| Goals | A god keeps at most one active goal: its own words (at most 140 characters, `MAX_GOAL_LENGTH`) and one target. The goal ends only when the god declares it achieved, failed, or abandoned, or sets a new one, which ends the old one as abandoned; an end is applied before a set. The rules record a goal and never judge it: they do not check the target's life or reach and never end a goal themselves. A goal is private to its god, and a change commits whatever becomes of the action it rides on | Default |
| Prompt caps | Code constants beside the memory and feeling caps, not content tunables: a prompt shows a god's 6 most salient memories (`MAX_REMEMBERED`), 5 strongest feelings (`MAX_FEELINGS`), its 5 newest own committed actions (`MAX_OWN_ACTIONS`), and the 4 newest entries of its goal's history (`MAX_GOAL_HISTORY`). Repeats are collapsed before those caps apply: at most 2 reports to one listener among its own actions and 2 told memories from one teller (`MAX_REPEATED_REPORTS`), the newest, so one repeated voice cannot crowd out what else it did or heard (its goal's history is not collapsed). Prayers are listed first in the prompt, ahead of the scene, memories, and its own actions, each with the next step toward answering it. The service reads the newest 20 events a god authored (`OWN_EVENT_WINDOW`, four times the actions shown) so a goal's history can reach back past the actions shown. Largest prompts of the M2 gate runs stay near 4.3 to 4.8 thousand characters on the 4K-token context. A prompt lists the open petitions addressed to the god within a budget of 3,000 characters (`PRAYERS_BUDGET_CHARS`, beside `DIGEST_BUDGET_CHARS`; 2,400 before a prayer gained its refusal, strike-the-mortal, and redress choices), because Ollama silently drops the start of a prompt, the system instructions, once it passes about 4,090 tokens, with no warning. Prayers a live practice names (an open offer, or an accepted term that names the prayer) come first and are never cut, then the newest; the rest become one line, `and N more prayers to you`. With the seven-god cast the busiest prompt (Poseidon at tick 400) was 2,435 prompt tokens on qwen3-8b-4k. The world keeps every petition: a hidden prayer is still answered, lapses, and counts as before, and the schema, the parser, and the openings name only the prayers shown | Default |

| Prompt cap | A god's whole request (instructions, a blank line, the prompt) is capped at run time: `PROMPT_TOKEN_CAP` is 3,000 tokens (`practices.ts`), counted in characters at the smallest characters-a-token ratio over the role's planned route, offline filter applied. `MODEL_RATIOS` (`prompt-cap.ts`, keyed by the router's model id): `granite3.3-8b-4k` 2.75, `qwen3-8b-4k` 3.34; a model not in the table uses `DEFAULT_RATIO` 2.75, no higher than any measured ratio. The ratios sit under the densest request seen on local Ollama. The unattended hour of 2026-10-08 measured granite3.3 at 2.85–3.35 and qwen3 at 3.34–3.78; the rerun with the cap (granite3.3-8b-4k, 245 requests matched to their trace rows) measured 2.807 at the densest, 3.124 median and 3.348 at the loosest, and its worst real over estimated was 1.015: one id-dense Hades request of 8,427 characters was 3,002 real tokens against an estimate of 2,957 at the earlier 2.85. 2.75 counts that request at 3,065, over the cap, and the limit becomes 8,250 characters. The cap sheds recent events, own actions, memories and feelings, then unprotected prayers, and refills what still fits (a goal-history row that no shown memory or own action backs is the first unit of the memories tier, restored last of them; the digest's offer opening points to the kept prayer's own "set terms" line instead of repeating its object); the instructions, persona, goal and journey, the god's obligations, prayers a live practice names, and a causal set (the newest memory, the evidence behind the demand opening shown first, one answerable prayer) are never shed. A turn whose floor and set are still over the cap is not sent (`prompt-over-cap`), and the router refuses any send over the cap times the ratio, cutting a retry's correction note to the room left. The whole-prompt character guards of the earlier prompt measures (10,500 and 11,000) are replaced by this cap; the section budgets stand. Measured 2026-10-08, counted at 2.85 (the ratio before the rerun above), local Ollama 0.34.4, `granite3.3-8b-4k` (`num_ctx` 4096), thinking off, the god's schema as `format` (`tools/probes/god-latency`, `capture-capped.ts` and `measure-capped.ts`, results in its README): 21 capped requests of the seven gods in an aged world and two crowded ones (8,507–8,550 characters, estimates 2,985–3,000 when the cap bit), each read with the model unloaded first. The largest real `prompt_eval_count` was 2,816 (pass line: every one at or under 3,000), and real over estimated ran 0.85–0.94 (median 0.91), 3.04–3.35 characters a token, so the estimate never undercounted and runs 6–15% over on these prompts; the busiest request per god repeated to the same count. The count is the whole request whether or not a cache serves it in this build (a resend reports the same count with a prefill of 65–80 ms), and the router's `/v1` route reports the same count as the native one. Warm, sequential across the gods with the tick moving: wall 11.0 s median and 14.8 s p95 in steady state (15.2 s and 18.9 s for the first round after a wipe), on a machine at load 2.6–15.5, against the hour's 8.5 s under 3,000 tokens and 16.4 s above 3,500 | Default |
| Petitions | A mortal prays at the altar about something that happened to it that it knows of, to one god, asking for help or for punishment of an offender it knows who owns a building. It knows a cause only through a memory of the cause event (witnessed, or told and citing it) that names the offender, through a memory of a loss it noticed (no offender), through its own unmet need or grudge, or through its own stolen or spoiled stock; never through the global log. A loss it noticed has no known offender, so it asks for help. Each cause leads to at most one petition, and a mortal holds at most one open petition per resource or building: while its own petition about food, say, or about one of its buildings is open, a new cause about the same resource or building is still a cause but is not prayed about, and once that petition is answered or lapses a later cause can lead to a prayer. A cause stays prayable for `causePrayableTicks` (150). A mortal prays at most once per `prayerCooldownTicks` (20) and walks home within that cooldown. Praying ranks below repair, production, and trades and above idle gathering. It prays to the god it weighs most: its affinity toward the god plus the god's standing at the place the mortal lives (its home, else where it stands), standing being capped at the affinity limit; on a tie, to the god that has received the fewest petitions, then by id | Default |
| Favour | A mortal's favour toward a god is its affinity plus the god's standing at its home: there is no separate favour score. A god's standing at a place is kept per god and place, moves only when a contest for the place closes, a defection is recorded, or a settlement is performed or breached there, and is held within the affinity limit either way. An answer is a private divine act, from which the tick derives a sign memory (a kindness by the god toward the petitioner), the worship, and the affinity rise, all through the relationship rules; a lapse derives a harm memory, an affinity fall, and a grudge. Reports and legends already move affinity through their claims, so favour reflects everything a mortal knows about a god. A dead petitioner gets no sign and nothing is derived | Default |
| Practice endings | A thread's ending changes the gods, from sourced motifs (`content/greek/lore/motifs.json`). A fulfilment raises the demander's affinity toward the performer by 1; a breach lowers the wronged party's by 2 and adds a grudge; a refusal lowers the refused party's affinity toward the refuser by 1 (`refusalAffinity`) with no grudge; an expiry, a withdrawal, and a death change no feeling. Both living parties remember each ending, as a memory of salience 7. A breach of a sworn term costs the one who swore 3 divinity (`oathDivinityLoss`, never more than it holds) and the divine capability, which closes Olympus to it, for 100 ticks (`oathAccessTicks`); an event restores it. A breach or a performance at a place changes the god's standing there by 1 (`standingDelta`), for good and within the affinity limit. A demand may stake a transformation: its breacher changes form and capabilities, and keeps identity, memory, and relationships. Only the god who must perform may swear. In `practiceBalance` and `memoryBalance`, strictly parsed | Default |
| Contests for favour | Gods whose pack names a rivalry contest a place's people: a contest runs 300 ticks (`contestWindowTicks`, five minutes: a god takes a turn about once a minute at seven gods), a rival's act can be contested for 120 ticks after it was done (`contestActTicks`), the world keeps the newest 48 acts (`contestLedgerMax`), and a closed contest moves each god's standing at the place by 1 (`contestStanding`, in the units of the affinity a mortal weighs it against; standing is held within the affinity limit). A bless or a legend weighs 1 for the god with each mortal it reached and a strike weighs 1 against it with each mortal at the struck place; a mortal favours the god it weighs strictly more, and only if that is positive, and only the living who still belong to the place (home, else where they stand) count at the close; the god more mortals favour wins, and no favour or an equal number expires the contest with no standing change, as does a place whose people have left. A prompt shows a god at most 600 characters about contests (`CONTESTS_BUDGET_CHARS`): the contests it is in, and the newest rival acts it may open one over, as choices, the rest counted | Default |
| Answers | A petition is answered by the named god's strike on an operational building the offender owns (answering every open punish petition against that offender), or by a bless that names one help petition, with the god standing with the petitioner and paying `blessDivinityCost` (2) divinity. A bless grants only that petition's need: `blessPlanks` (3) planks for a damaged building, the lost amount of a resource up to `blessResourceCap` (4) for stolen or spoiled stock, or `blessResourceAmount` (2) of the resource for an unmet need. The repair routine mends a building it was granted planks for first. The answer window is inclusive and lasts `answerWindowTicks` (150; owner, 2026-10-05, down from 250): the lapse is shortened so that every devotion (2 to 4) can defect inside a 300-tick episode, and ignoring prayers costs a god sooner. It still covers one trip from the great hall to a petition target (4 moves) plus the answering action at the slowest measured god pace of one action per 25 ticks (125 ticks), but no longer twice that, so a god at that pace has no spare round trip. An answer on a petition's last tick wins over its lapse | Default |
| Patrons | Every mortal has a stored patron god, seeded from its authored devotion (content parse refuses a mortal without one) and kept in the world state, its codec, and the export. Only a recorded defection changes it. A prayer about a wrong, a grudge, a harm, or a need, hunger included, goes to the patron; no god in the cast has food in its domain. A trouble with no mortal doer (fire, spoiled stock, a director's theft) goes to the god `rules.troubleKinds` names for its kind (fire: Hephaestus, spoilage: Hera, theft: Hermes, and each domain trouble below; sourced in the god profiles from 2026-10-05, Unit 5); a fire a god's strike started is that god's harm and goes to the patron. A mortal built outside a content pack, with no patron, still prays by affinity plus standing as the Petitions and Favour rows say, which the patron supersedes for every authored mortal (owner, 2026-10-05) | Default |
| Punishing a mortal | A strike may name a living mortal as well as a building (owner, 2026-10-05). The world, not the god, chooses what is taken: the mortal's most valuable carried good by value per unit, then the larger holding, then the name that sorts first, up to `strikeGoodsCap` (2) units, a petition tunable a positive whole number. The cap equals `blessResourceAmount`, so a punishment costs about what one answered prayer gives. The goods are taken and given to no one. A mortal carrying nothing is still struck: the harm is credited to the god with nothing taken. A strike on a mortal answers every open punish petition against it addressed to the striker, and a `punish` request no longer needs the offender to own a building: a grudge against a living mortal asks for its punishment (a grudge against a god or a dead mortal still asks for nothing). The struck mortal always knows who struck it (a harm cause naming the god, whatever the strike's salience) and prays about it to its own patron ahead of any need; a harm that took goods asks for them back, one that took nothing asks the patron to make the god answer. A prayer addressed to a god is a cause that god knows, so a patron's demand against the punisher may cite it; no other god learns of it, and a god never demands of itself. The strike's memory is `salience_mortal-struck` (5) | Default |
| Refusing a prayer | A god may refuse an open prayer addressed to it, inside its answer window: it closes, and the petitioner remembers the refusal as harm by the god, a sign of salience 6, which costs the god the same affinity and grudge a lapse does (`harmAffinity`, 2, and one grudge). A refused prayer never lapses. The world offers the move as the `refuse` proposal; the gods' prompts offer it from the prompt unit onward | Default |
| Defection | After any prayer ends in a tick (answered, refused, or lapsed) the world checks the petitioner's affinity for its patron against `defectionAffinity` (1, a petition tunable, a positive whole number): below it, a mortal takes as its patron the last other god that answered one of its prayers, or keeps its patron when none has, with no cooldown, so it may later go back to a god that answered it. The threshold is 1 so that a mortal defects once it feels nothing or worse toward its patron (affinity 0 or less): a devotion starts at 2 to 4, and a lapse or a refusal costs 2 (`harmAffinity`), so one ignored prayer turns a devotion of 2 and two turn any authored devotion. A prayer lapses `answerWindowTicks` (150) after it opens, so in a 300-tick episode every devotion can defect: a devotion of 2 needs one ignored prayer, and 3 and 4 need two, opened by tick 49 (the prayer cooldown is 20). With a god other than the patron having answered it, the SC6 test shows devotions of 2, 3, and 4 defecting at ticks 175, 197, and 197, each inside the episode. The `patron-changed` event cites the ignored prayers (newest first, at most five) and the answered one. Only the god lost and the god gained remember it (`patronage` memory, `salience_patronage` 8): the mortal, its home, and the other god; at salience 0 it still defects but no god remembers. The two gods' standing at the mortal's home moves by `standingDelta` (1), the god lost falling and the god gained rising. Standing is otherwise moved only by a closing contest or a performed or breached settlement: answered and unanswered prayers do not move it, since affinity already prices them and a standing swing from every prayer would drown a contest's result (owner, 2026-10-05: standing stays moved only by events, defections, contests, and settlements, and prayers never move it; a narrower reading than the plan's counts of worshippers and answered prayers). Standing no longer moves the prayers of a mortal with a patron, only those of one with none. The god lost may contest the defector's home with the god gained, rivals or not: the contest rests on its own `patronage` memory, needs no perceived act and no act young enough, can be cited only by the god lost and only once, opens only if no contest is open at the place (a defection there is recorded and opens nothing), and closes by the contest rules | Default |
| Seasons | The season is `floor(tick / seasonTicks) mod 4` (spring at tick 0, then summer, autumn, winter), so replay and catch-up reach it with no state; a `season-turned` event on each boundary tick records the turn (a pack with a trouble table has seasons; one without has none). `seasonTicks` is 200, a petition tunable and a positive whole number: a tick is one simulated second, so a season is 3 minutes 20 seconds and a year 800 ticks, any 300-tick episode holds at least one turn (200 is at most 300) and the one-hour run's 3,600 ticks turn 18 times (R13) | Default |
| Domain troubles | Each god has troubles the world brings in its domain, sourced in its profile (each domain attested, each trouble a labelled invention) and drawn by `rules.troubles`: an effect (a **resource** trouble takes some of the first good in its list a mortal carries; a **building** trouble damages one of its buildings, or any operational owned one, and its owner is the afflicted), and per-mille odds each tick in each season (3 in the seasons the sources give, none otherwise). `rules.troubleKinds` names the one god each trouble, and each base kind, belongs to: spoilage to Hera (household stores), the director's theft to Hermes (no mortal thief), fire to Hephaestus. Zeus: squall (a building, autumn and winter), lightning fire (wood or food, summer); Hera: roof leak (food, autumn and winter), damp (cloth or food, winter); Athena: loom break (the loom house, winter), tool flaw (tools or planks, spring and autumn); Hermes: vanished goods (fish, olives, wine, or food, spring and summer), crossing loss (fish, wool, ore, or olives, spring and autumn); Hephaestus: forge flare (the forge, summer), cracked tools (tools or ore, autumn and winter); Poseidon: harbour surge (the fish landing, autumn and winter), quake (any building, spring and autumn); Hades: hoard swallowed (coin, autumn and winter), dig collapse (ore, spring and autumn), ore seam lost (ore or tools, winter), none of which needs a death. Sky weather and lightning go to Zeus, sea damage and quakes to Poseidon; harvest failure and plague are out of scope. A trouble is one `trouble` event (the afflicted mortal, the trouble, its god, the season, whether the season or the floor fired it, what it took), takes at most `troubleLossCap` (2, what one answered prayer gives) units, and is a loss the afflicted always knows and prays about, to the trouble's god and not its patron, ahead of no prayer and behind none about the same good (a trouble's prayer waits behind no open prayer about the goods it took, since that one goes to another god). **The floor:** in every window of `troubleFloorTicks` (150, half a 300-tick episode, so every episode holds a whole window) a god with no trouble yet in it has one at a tick the persisted PRNG picks: each tick the chance is one in the ticks the window has left, so the last tick is certain; it falls on the god's trouble the season favours (each weighs its odds plus one) that can happen, and on none when none can. **Draw order:** credit judgments, wrongs, revenge, then the gods' troubles (the season's draw over troubles in id order, then each god's floor), then fire, then the director. **Evidence (on the occasional-hunger town, untuned):** over five seeds, a 300-tick day of the authored town brings 14 to 17 troubles (12 to 14 by the floor, 1 to 5 by season: 14 of 78 by season), every god's in every 150-tick window on every seed (SC2). A trouble's prayer is opened for 54 of the 78 (the rest are late in the episode or behind the afflicted's prayer cooldown and its newer causes), and every god is prayed to about one of its own over the five seeds, though not in every episode: 3 of the 35 god-episodes (Hades twice, Hephaestus once) have none, so the success criterion that every god receives a prayer per episode is met in 32 of 35 and not tuned toward. Season-sourced troubles are under a fifth of all because 3 per mille over a 200-tick season is under one a trouble a season: the floor is what the guarantee rests on, the season's odds what varies it | Default |
| Wrongs between mortals | A mortal's authored temperament (`greedy`, `quarrelsome`, `proud`, `honest`; absent means honest) sets its per-mille odds each tick of each wrong, in `rules.temperamentOdds`, and an open unmet need multiplies them by `wrongNeedMultiplier` (2), capped at certain. Authored odds: greedy theft 2, cheating 1, an unpaid debt or a broken agreement 150 (the odds of failing a credit it could keep) and revenge 20; quarrelsome feud 2, debt and agreement 50, revenge 40; proud feud 1, debt and agreement 30, revenge 60; honest none. A theft takes a co-located mortal's most valuable good (by value per unit, never coin), a cheat takes its coin, a feud spoils its most valuable good, each up to `wrongLossCap` (2, the amount one answered prayer gives); a wrongdoer draws only outside `wrongCooldownTicks` (30) of its last wrong. Every wrong is one `wrong` event: wrongdoer, victim, kind, loss, the temperament and need behind it. The victim always knows it and who did it, and prays to its patron, a punish request naming the wrongdoer even when it owns no building; the grudge the memory of it leaves is not a second prayer. A god never learns of a wrong by standing near it: only the victim's prayer tells it (so a god's witnessed memories and its scene stay as they were). **Credit:** a mortal with an open unmet need and no funds is sold the good by a co-located mortal that holds it, the price owed within `creditDeadlineTicks` (100); one with funds and no seller buys in advance from a co-located producer of the good that is out of stock, the good owed within the same time. At the deadline the one who owes pays or delivers if it holds it, unless its temperament's odds of failing a credit say it does not (one draw, only when above 0), and otherwise fails: an unpaid debt (the buyer) or a broken agreement (the seller), each a wrong whose loss is the price or the goods. **Revenge:** after its prayer about a wrong was refused or lapsed (a refusal counts as unanswered), the victim may take one feud against the wrongdoer, drawing once a tick against its temperament's revenge odds, while the wrongdoer is alive and beside it with goods to spoil and within `revengeWindowTicks` (300, the 150-tick answer window after which a prayer lapses, and 150 more); out of reach it does nothing and records nothing. A revenge links the wrong it answers, is never avenged in turn, and a wrong is avenged once. **Draw order:** credit judgments, wrongs, revenge, then the gods' troubles (Domain troubles row), then fire, then the director, each over mortals in id order. **Measured (2026-10-05), not tuned:** the odds above were set before hunger became occasional and are left as authored. On the town as authored, with the 150-tick prayer lapse and the occasional-hunger food model, a 300-tick day on five seeds gave 13, 9, 12, 11, and 14 wrongs (59), of which 12, 6, 10, 10, and 13 (51) were between mortals of different patrons, 7 of them revenges, and 42 credit trades, all of them goods now and the price later: feud 22, theft 18, unpaid debts 10, cheating 9. A credit is extended only on a shortfall the buyer still has at that step, so a need a proposal or income already met this tick is sold nothing. The cross-patron count holds at least 3 on every one of those seeds, and with every mortal fed (no hunger, no food credit) it still does. **Prepaid credit:** the trigger stays as built: a buyer with funds and no seller prepays only a co-located producer that is out of stock. Under the current food model that rarely or never starts (a food producer restocks within a tick, and a mortal who finds no seller walks off), so a broken agreement may simply not occur in a run; no trade was prepaid on any of the five seeds. This is left as it is (owner, 2026-10-05); the real simulation runs far longer than a 300-tick day, and the odds are not tuned before the system is built | Default |
| Director | On its own clock, every `directorIntervalTicks` (120) ticks since it last fired, a world step causes one trouble among living mortals: theft, spoiled stock, or a fire in a victim's building, chosen from the persisted PRNG, victims in id order, preferring mortals at places where something is open (an open thread's term place or a party's home, or a contest no god has served yet) and falling back to all mortals when nothing is open, with the director recorded as its cause. Only its own trouble moves its clock (`director.lastFireTick`): strikes, fires, trades, blessings, answered petitions, prayers, goals, and god talk do not (R15; the retired `directorQuietTicks` window reset on any consequential event, so blessing gods could hold the director off for ever, which AE6 forbids). It skips when fewer than two mortals are eligible, and never undoes damage. The table sends its theft to Hermes, its spoiled stock to Hera, and its fire to Hephaestus | Default |
| Goal gate | Setting the identical goal again records nothing (compared trimmed, lowercased, whitespace-collapsed). Ending a goal as achieved or failed is always allowed. | Hunger | A mortal eats once every `economyBalance.mealIntervalTicks` ticks (20 in the Greek pack), offset by its id so the town does not sit down together; with the key unset it eats whenever it holds food, as before. At mealtime a meal outranks every ordinary trade, production, and prayer whatever the mortal's drive weights; only repair, answering a god, and an offering near its deadline outrank it. Only a food producer (a mortal that gathers food) sells food, one meal to whoever asks, never pushed onto a buyer who is not hungry; its unsold food is not an unmet need. A mortal short of food where no producer stands waits one meal interval, less a tick, for one to turn up, then walks to the nearest producer holding food, keeping its shortfall open until it is fed. Measured on a scripted 300-tick day with no god acting (seed 7), before and after: "cannot get food" lines 1,277 (962 no-seller, 314 no-funds, 1 no-buyer) to 38 (3 no-seller, 35 no-funds); food prayers 37 of 44 to 6 of 17; food gathered 920 and eaten 978 to gathered 2,274 and eaten 265; 11 pairs of mortals trading food both ways to none. | God turn order | Seven gods share one model, one inference at a time, and a god with a turn in the journal waits. The next turn goes to a god that owes an accepted obligation (a term it must perform, or a boon on a prayer it set terms on), whatever the deadline, the earliest term deadline first; then to a god a thread is waiting on for an answer; then to the rest in id order from the god served last. Ties inside a tier go by that cursor. A prayer or a contest raises nothing, and neither the clock nor a built prompt is an input: the order is read from world state by the rule the practice digest sorts its rows by, so a god the scheduler calls owing is one whose prompt leads with a YOU OWE row. No god offered at every pick is passed over more than 7 picks in a row (`SKIP_CAP`, one full round of seven; a code constant beside the prompt caps, not a content tunable). The bound is kept for every god at once, not only the one that reaches it: a pick is the first god in tier order whose service still leaves every other god a way to be served within the cap, and a god at the cap goes first, ahead of every tier. With seven gods that leaves one spare pick in eight, so a god that always owes takes at most two of eight turns and the others one, in the order the tiers give; with more gods offered than a round holds, the god passed over most goes first. The cursor and the skip counts live in the service's memory and start over on a restart; the world never reads them | Default |
| God travel | Owner's rules of 2026-10-04. A god has one way to move, `travel(to)`, and it replaces `move` and `realm-transition` for gods (mortal routines still use both). It names any place a route reaches from where the god stands, without entering a place whose capability the god lacks; its prompt lists those places, nearest first with their steps, in place of its exits. Travel is that turn's action. A validated travel stores one journey for the god (its destination, on the `journey-started` event) and the first hop happens on the tick it is applied, so a one-hop trip costs what a move costs. Every tick the world advances every journey by one hop: it computes the next hop from where the god stands (never a stored route), turns it into a `move`, or a `realm-transition` with `via` when the hop leaves the realm, and runs it through the same validators and reducers as any other. Routes cross realms only through authored crossings, which the validators enforce. No model is asked and no divinity is spent for a hop. A journey ends in exactly one way, recorded by one `journey-ended` event: `arrived`; `refused`, when a hop is refused or no route remains (a place on the way became closed to the god, a transport is unusable, the god died), with the world's reason, and a refused hop moves nothing; or `replaced`, when the god commits its next proposal, whatever it is, or a new travel. There are no retries. A proposal that is rejected commits nothing and does not end a journey. Each hop is an ordinary `entity-moved` or `realm-transitioned` event, witnessed as today; the journey events, and so the destination, are private to the god, like its goals. A god on a journey takes no turn until it ends, unless a thread waits on its answer (the scheduler's awaited signal); owing alone does not call it back, and a god kept out is not passed over. If it then commits anything the journey ends as replaced; if it waits, the journey runs on. The prompt of a god on a journey says where it is going and how far it has to go, and, once a journey is over and the god has done nothing of its own since, how it ended. The journey is one field of the world state (`journeys`, by god), rebuilt from the log like goals, and part of the archive's projection check | Default |

## Numerical parameters

The one-hour catch-up cap, seven-day trace retention, checkpoint interval, and combat timeout are starting parameters.
Put them in documented configuration rather than bury them in prompts.
Probes and usability evidence can change them without altering the accepted product boundary.

Population, exact models, image-job latency, and minimum OS versions remain probe outputs.
Start the population experiment at seven gods and twenty ordinary inhabitants, then publish measured limits.
Do not reduce the seven-god content roster to hide an inference bottleneck.

## Experiments and event history

A snapshot copy supports a manual comparison between two model or prompt configurations.
It does not promise identical model outputs.
World playback uses recorded outcomes, assets, and behavior versions without new inference.
Changing retention must explain which playback intervals or diagnostic details will become unavailable.
